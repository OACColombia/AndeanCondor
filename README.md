# Andean Condor perception

This is the repository of the analyzes of the manuscript in preparation "Conflict and coexistence between people and the world's largest scavenger bird", by Juan Sebastián Restrepo, Orlando Acevedo-Charry, Rubén Pineida, Sebastián Kohn, Diego Bamontes, Fabricio Narváez, Paul Monar-Barragán, & Vanessa Hull. This paper aims to understand human-vulture perception of conflict regarding the role of the Andean Condor (*Vultur gryphus*) as a potential predator. The specific aims were: 

* To examine local perceptions of the negative impacts of condors as potential predator of livestock, and analyze how these perceptions relate to socio-demographic and psychological factors, livestock management practices, and experience or attitude by farmers.

* To examine local perceptions of the condor's contributions to farmers: negative due to perception of harmfulness, positive due to perceived benefits and values, or a combination of both, and analyze how these perceptions relate to socio-demographic and psychological factors, livestock management practices, and experience or attitude by farmers (including perception of attacking livestock).

* To explore how people's perceptions of the costs and benefits associated with condors, and the previous exposure to environmental workshops, influence coexistence-based conservation behaviors (evaluate conservation behaviors).
  
* To assess whether field observations support the reported perceptions of condor-related damage

A further work will assess attitudes (feelings, disappear, population preference change) as response variables, but in this study we focused on perceptions as response variables.
